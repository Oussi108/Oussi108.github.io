About Me

I am an M.Sc. student in Computer Science at Nanjing University of Information Science and Technology (NUIST), China, and an engineer with a State Engineering Degree in Information Systems Engineering from SUPMTI (Morocco).

My current research focuses on practical forecasting questions: Which learned solar power forecasts actually outperform smart reference benchmarks across lead times, and when does higher forecast accuracy translate into real operational decisions and economic savings?

With a strong background as a software engineer (C#/.NET, Java, Flutter, Django, and Distributed Systems), I apply engineering best practices—such as pre-registered evaluation protocols, byte-identical reproducibility, checksum manifests, and pytest verification suites—to scientific machine learning. I am currently working on research publications in solar forecasting and battery optimization.

Solar Forecasting Research (Working on Papers)
Working on Paper 2026 Ongoing Study

Reference specification, model updating and calibration population in hourly photovoltaic power forecasting

El Houcine Oussi and Ling Tan — Nanjing University of Information Science and Technology (NUIST)

Investigates how the performance advantage of learned models (LightGBM, GRU neural networks, and compact Transformers) shifts across lead times of 2, 4, 6, and 12 hours under reference specification choices, retraining schedules, and prediction interval calibration. Evaluated on three testbeds: Alice Springs (DKASC), Hong Kong (HKUST), and Colorado (NREL PVDAQ 9068) under a pre-registered protocol. Currently working on the publication.

Models: LightGBM, GRU, Transformers Datasets: DKASC, HKUST, NREL PVDAQ View GitHub Code & Benchmark Zenodo DOI
Working on Paper Operational Battery Scheduling

Operational Value of Probabilistic Solar Forecasts in Behind-the-Meter Battery Scheduling

El Houcine Oussi and Collaborators — Nanjing University of Information Science and Technology

Examines when probabilistic day-ahead solar forecasts provide measurable economic value in behind-the-meter home-battery scheduling. Benchmarked against 91,290 simulated scenarios across 40 real Ausgrid residential households. Demonstrated that scheduling systems leveraging full forecast distributions consistently reduce electricity costs across all 40 households. Currently working on the publication.
